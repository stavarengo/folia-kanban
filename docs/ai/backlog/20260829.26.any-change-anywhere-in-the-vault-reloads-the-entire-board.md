# Any change anywhere in the vault reloads the entire board

## What happens

Finding 04-09 of the audit of 2026-08-29 ([section 04](../reports/20260829.what-obsidian-already-provides/04.vault-files-and-metadata.md)).

`src/obsidian/vaultRepo.ts:766` subscribes to `vault.on("modify" | "create" | "delete" | "rename")` and calls `fireVault(f.path)` (`:757`), which checks the path only against the plugin's own recent-writes guard before scheduling a full `loadBoard`. Nothing filters by the board note or by the card folder. Editing an unrelated note anywhere in the vault, or any background process that touches files, reloads the whole board: every card note read again, for a change that cannot possibly affect it.

Two things make this worth more than its performance cost. Every reload is another chance for two of them to land out of order, which is the hazard entry 20260828.02 describes. And finding 04-14, about the wall-clock window that filters the board's own writes out of the event stream, is only affordable to improve once the event volume is small — the report reads that guard as fine as it stands, and notes it becomes worth revisiting after this.

The one thing to be careful about is named in the finding: a rename that moves a note *into* the card folder must be tested on the incoming path, and a delete on the old one.

## How to observe

Open a board, then edit any note elsewhere in the vault and watch the board reload.

## Expected outcome

A vault change that cannot affect the open board does not reload it.
