# Adding a rule to a populated column strands its cards

## What happens

Giving a column a `filter:` rule turns it into a lane: it stops drawing its own status bucket and draws by the rule instead. Every write that would put a card into a lane it does not match is now refused with the reason — a drag, the add-card controls, the panel's **Status** field, a subtask's column picker, a column deletion, and the MCP `move_card` / `create_card` tools all ask the same question first.

One path does not, and it is the one that can strand a whole column at once: the column editor (`src/ui/ColumnEditModal.tsx`, written through `updateColumn` in `src/ui/App.tsx`). Typing a rule into a column that already holds cards makes every card in its bucket that the rule rejects stop being drawn there. Nothing is refused and nothing is said.

Those cards are not lost — the board's first ordinary column takes them in (`fallbackColumnOf` in `src/model/lanes.ts`), which is the same rescue a hand-edited `status` gets. But they move without being asked about, and if the column being given a rule *was* the first ordinary column, the cards previously rescued there lose their home too.

## Why it matters

It is the last way to reach the state the refusals exist to prevent, and the only one that reaches it for many cards at once. It is also the moment the user is most able to act on the information: they are editing that column, and "17 of these 20 cards do not match this rule" is exactly what they would want to know before saving.

## What to work out

The refusal shape does not transfer — declining to save the rule would be worse than the problem, since a rule that matches nothing today may be exactly what the user is building towards. What fits is a warning at the point of editing, in the modal, counting the cards the rule would stop drawing, with the save still available. Whether it also offers to move them somewhere is the open part.
