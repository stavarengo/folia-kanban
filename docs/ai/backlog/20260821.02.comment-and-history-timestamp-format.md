# Comments and History write their timestamp in square brackets, where an italic prefix would read better

## What happens

Every comment and every history line the plugin writes into a card note puts the date and time inside square brackets, like `- [2026-08-21 11:49] comment 1`.

The brackets never appear inside the board's own detail panel. The parser strips them and the panel renders the timestamp on its own, in a muted `.folia-ts` span. They only show when the note is read as plain Markdown, which is to say whenever someone opens the card outside the board, in Obsidian's normal editor or anywhere else. There the timestamp is undifferentiated plain text sitting in bracket noise, right next to Obsidian syntax that brackets normally mean something in.

## How to see it

This is the example Rafa pointed at, copied here in full because the original lives in the repository's example vault at `examples/feature-showcase/Cards/Draft the announcement post.md` and will keep changing as that vault is used. What matters is the shape of the last two sections.

```markdown
---
status: next
order: 1
priority: B
due: 2026-07-10
tags: [marketing, writing]
area: marketing
---

# Draft the announcement post

Forum + Reddit + the changelog. Lead with the "plain Markdown, no lock-in" angle.

## Comments
- [2026-08-21 11:49] comment 1

## History
- [2026-08-21 11:49] Comment added
```

## Desired outcome

The date and time should be visually distinct when the note is read as Markdown, italicised and underlined, and the brackets should go away. The format Rafa asked for, verbatim:

```markdown
- _2026-08-21 11:49 CET:_ content
```

Two things in that request are worth settling with him before implementing, because the sample and the description do not fully agree and one field is new.

The sample produces italics only, and underline is the expensive half of the ask. In Obsidian, `_text_` and `__text__` are just alternates of the asterisk syntax, italic and bold respectively, and there is no native Markdown underline at all; Obsidian's own documentation names raw `<u>text</u>` as the way to get one. Italic and underlined together therefore means writing an HTML tag into every comment and history line the plugin appends. Confirm with Rafa whether that is worth it, or whether the italics in his sample were the real intent.

The sample carries a timezone label, `CET`, which today's timestamps do not have at all. The stamp the plugin writes is local time with no zone marker. Adding one is a separate decision from the punctuation change: it affects what gets written from here on, and it says nothing about the zone of the timestamps already on disk.

## What this touches

The on-disk shape of these lines is read and written in several places, all in `src/model/card.ts`, and they have to agree with each other:

- `appendComment` and `appendHistory` write the bracketed form.
- `parseTimestamped`, via the `TS_LINE_RE` pattern, is the only thing that reads it back; a line it does not match loses its timestamp and keeps only its text.
- `setTimestampedText` edits a line while deliberately preserving its `[timestamp] ` prefix byte for byte, and `removeTimestampedLine` deletes by position.

The panel is a consumer of the format too. `CardDetail.tsx` renders whatever `parseTimestamped` hands it straight into a `.folia-ts` span, for comments and for history alike, so any punctuation that becomes part of the captured timestamp shows up there verbatim. A trailing colon written into the file would surface as a stray colon in the panel.

Beyond the engine, the bracketed form appears in the test fixtures under `test/fixtures/`, in both example vaults under `examples/`, and, most importantly, in every card note any existing user has already accumulated. Those notes will not rewrite themselves, so the reading side has to keep making sense of them.
